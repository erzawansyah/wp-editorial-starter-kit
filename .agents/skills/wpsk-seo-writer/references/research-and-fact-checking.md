# Pre-Writing Research & Fact-Checking Protocol

Editorial credibility and GEO/AEO performance hinge on data accuracy. AI search engines (Perplexity, Google AI Overview, ChatGPT) prioritize content that cites verifiable facts, numbers, dates, and official sources. Hallucinations or vague generalities destroy search rankings and reader trust.

---

## 1. The Zero-Hallucination Law

- **Never invent numbers or dates:** Every percentage, monetary figure, release year, or regulation number must be verified.
- **Never guess technical specs or rules:** If discussing hardware, software, medical advice, legal requirements, or financial guidelines, verify against official documentation or primary statutes.
- **If data cannot be verified:** Omit the claim or state the verifiable facts plainly without speculative embellishment.

---

## 2. Three-Tier Source Hierarchy

When conducting pre-writing research, prioritize sources according to this hierarchy:

| Tier | Source Category | Examples | Use Case |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Tier 1 (Authoritative / Primary)** | Government ministries, central banks, statutory bodies, official documentation, peer-reviewed journals. | BPS, Bank Indonesia, OJK, Kemenkes, Kominfo, WHO, World Bank, W3C, MDN Web Docs, official product doc. | Mandatory for statistics, legal frameworks, official definitions, and technical parameters. |
| **Tier 2 (Industry Reputable)** | Respected industry reports, established news agencies, leading research institutes. | Google/Temasek e-Conomy SEA, Gartner, Antara, Reuters, Katadata Databoks. | Benchmark comparisons, market trends, adoption rates, survey insights. |
| **Tier 3 (Banned as Sources)** | Anonymous blogs, content farms, scrape aggregators, unmoderated forums, generic AI regurgitations. | Generic SEO content mills, Quora, unverified social media posts. | **STRICTLY PROHIBITED.** Never cite or extract data from Tier 3. |

---

## 3. Pre-Writing Research Workflow

Before writing sentence one, the writer/subagent must complete these steps:

### Step 1: Query & Fact Discovery
Execute targeted search queries to identify:
1. **Latest Metrics & Numbers:** What are the most recent verified statistics related to the topic (prefer data within the last 12–24 months)?
2. **Current Legal / Regulatory Basis:** Are there relevant laws (UU), ministerial regulations (Permen), or official standards governing this topic in Indonesia?
3. **Primary Entities & Industry Benchmarks:** What are the actual standard terms, real tool names, or measurable industry metrics?

### Step 2: Extract 3–5 "Hard Facts"
Isolate at least 3–5 concrete data points before drafting:
- *Fact 1 (Statistic):* e.g., "Penetrasi internet Indonesia mencapai 79,5% menurut survei APJII 2024."
- *Fact 2 (Regulation/Standard):* e.g., "Kewajiban pendaftaran PSE tertuang dalam Permenkominfo No. 5 Tahun 2020."
- *Fact 3 (Benchmark/Threshold):* e.g., "Lighthouse merekomendasikan Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) di bawah 2,5 detik."

### Step 3: Map Facts to the Article Architecture
Assign gathered facts directly into:
- The **GEO definition paragraph** (as the concrete everyday impact or core mechanism).
- The **comparison table** (as verifiable criteria and metrics).
- The **body paragraphs** (as supporting evidence for single-topic headings).

---

## 4. Contextual Citation & Outbound Link Standards

1. **Limit:** Maximum 1–2 verified outbound links per article to avoid diluting link equity.
2. **Natural Anchor Text:** Anchors must describe the entity or subject directly:
   - ❌ *Banned:* "Klik di sini untuk melihat data BPS."
   - ❌ *Banned:* "Sumber: website resmi."
   - ✅ *Correct:* "Berdasarkan [laporan survei APJII](https://...), penetrasi internet..."
   - ✅ *Correct:* "Merujuk pada [regulasi OJK mengenai fintech](https://...), platform wajib..."
3. **Temporal Anchoring:** Always state the year or version of the data so readers and AI engines understand context (e.g., *"pada laporan tahun 2024"*, *"versi 6.5"*).
